# Digital Product Brief — isitsafeformydog.com

**How to use this document:** paste into a fresh session for isitsafeformydog.com as a new work item. It defines intent, product structure, pricing, funnel placement, and success criteria. It is a specification, not a finished plan — expect to adjust once real Search Console and GA4 data is checked.

---

## 1. Strategic intent

This is the portfolio's **first-party revenue test**. The site already has compounding organic sessions and live Amazon Associates affiliate infrastructure. The purpose of this product is not primarily the revenue from this product. It is to establish a **conversion baseline** — sales per 1,000 sessions on high-intent pages — which then governs whether digital products are worth building anywhere else in the portfolio.

Secondary goals, in order:

1. Capture value from traffic that currently monetises at affiliate rates only.
2. Build an email list of dog owners (currently absent, and the most valuable long-term asset here).
3. Prove or disprove the "attach a paid product to an SEO asset" model cheaply, before applying it to the directory properties.

**Effort budget: two weeks maximum.** This is a validation instrument. Do not gold-plate it. If it underperforms, the sunk cost must be small enough to walk away from without regret.

## 2. Audience and buying psychology

The reader arrives in one of three states:

- **Mid-emergency** — dog has just eaten something. High anxiety, low patience, will not buy anything. Serve them fast and well.
- **Just-past-emergency** — dog is fine, owner is shaken. This is the buying moment. The emotion is "I never want to feel that again."
- **Preventative** — new puppy, new house, researching. Lower volume, higher intent, converts well.

The product must therefore be positioned as **preparedness, not treatment**. The pitch is: *the reference you wish had been on your fridge before it happened.*

## 3. Product structure

**Working title:** The Dog Safety Reference (test alternatives — "Emergency Dog Safety Kit" may convert better)

**Format:** PDF, roughly 30–45 pages, plus separate print-optimised single sheets. Must be readable on a phone in a panic and printable in black and white.

Contents, in priority order:

1. **The fridge chart** — one page, A–Z quick reference of common household toxins with a three-band severity indicator (call vet now / monitor / not a concern). This is the hero asset. Design it to be printed and stuck up.
2. **Triage flow** — "my dog ate X" decision tree. Ingestion → quantity relative to dog weight → symptom watch → action. Weight bands, not precise dosing.
3. **Vet call script** — exactly what information to have ready before dialling: weight, time of ingestion, estimated quantity, packaging/brand, current symptoms, existing conditions. A fillable page for the dog's own details.
4. **Emergency contacts sheet** — fillable. Own vet, out-of-hours clinic, national poison line numbers by country (US, UK, IE, CA, AU at minimum).
5. **Symptom reverse index** — "my dog is vomiting / lethargic / drooling" → plausible causes, ranked. This is the highest-value section and the hardest to fake.
6. **Room-by-room household audit** — kitchen, bathroom, garage, garden. Checklist format.
7. **Safe alternatives index** — what they *can* have. Ends the document on relief rather than fear.
8. **Seasonal hazards** — Christmas, Easter, summer BBQ, autumn (mushrooms, antifreeze).

### Non-negotiable content constraints

- **Every claim sourced.** Reuse the citation discipline already established on the site. Buyers of a safety reference are exactly the audience who will check.
- **No dosing guidance that could be used to self-treat.** No induced-vomiting protocols, no "you can give X mg of Y." The answer to a real ingestion is always a vet or a poison line.
- **"Not a substitute for veterinary care" must be prominent** — on the cover, in the front matter, and at the foot of every action-oriented page. This is both an ethical requirement and liability management.
- **Dated and versioned.** Include a "current as of" date and commit to an annual review. Buyers get updates free — this is a purchase justification, not a burden.

## 4. Pricing

- **Launch price: $15** (price in the dominant traffic currency — check GA4 geography first; this is likely US-majority, in which case USD, not EUR).
- Test $12 and $19 after 300+ sessions through each variant.
- Optional second tier at **$29**: adds the editable/fillable version, a printed-poster-ready A3 file, and a multi-dog household set. Build this only if tier one converts.
- Do not discount below $9. Below that the fixed transaction fee becomes a meaningful share of gross and it signals low value.

**Platform: Lemon Squeezy.** 5% + $0.50 as merchant of record, so EU VAT and US sales tax are handled and none of it touches Irish OSS obligations. On a $15 sale that's $1.25 versus roughly $2.00 on Gumroad. No storefront build required — hosted checkout, direct file delivery.

Set a **no-questions 30-day refund policy** and honour it instantly. Refund rate on a product like this should sit under 3%; anything higher is a product problem, not a customer problem.

## 5. Funnel and placement

**Free lead magnet:** the fridge chart alone, email-gated. This is the list-building engine and the top of the funnel. It should be genuinely useful on its own — giving away the best single page is the point.

**Email sequence:** 4–5 emails over two weeks. Deliver the chart, then a short sequence on the most-asked questions, then the paid reference offered once, plainly, without a countdown timer or fake scarcity.

**On-page placement:**

- Inline CTA placed **after the answer**, never before it. The user's question gets answered first, in full, for free. Selling above the answer damages both trust and rankings.
- Persistent but unobtrusive sidebar unit.
- Target the top 20 pages by impressions first (the existing impressions-vs-CTR prioritisation method applies directly here).
- No pop-ups, no exit-intent overlays, no interstitials. The site's asset is trust and Core Web Vitals; do not trade either for a marginal conversion bump.

**Do not** touch existing affiliate placements while testing. If product revenue comes at the cost of affiliate revenue, the test result is meaningless. Track both.

## 6. Measurement and decision thresholds

Primary metric: **sales ÷ sessions on pages carrying a CTA**, measured over a 90-day window.

| Result | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.1% | Product or positioning is wrong | Rework copy and placement once, then stop |
| 0.1–0.3% | Marginal | Test price and title variants; keep it running, don't expand |
| 0.3–0.7% | Working | Expand to all pages, build tier two, add more lead magnets |
| > 0.7% | Strong | Build the course version; apply this model to the directory sites |

Also track, by page cluster: which intent types convert. This tells you where the next product should point. And track email list growth rate independently — if the paid product fails but the list grows fast, the list is the win.

## 7. Anti-goals

- No membership, no login, no subscription. Delivery is a file.
- No community, no comments, no support commitment beyond a reply-to email address.
- No course, no video, until this converts above 0.7%.
- No AI-generated cover art or filler pages. The whole premise is that this is more trustworthy than what a stranger could prompt in an afternoon.
